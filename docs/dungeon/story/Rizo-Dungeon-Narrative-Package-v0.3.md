## 0. Authority

This addendum uses the same two categories as v0.1 and v0.2, plus one marker for things the owner deliberately left open.

**LOCKED CANON** — owner-adopted. Implementation may rely on it.

**PROPOSED** — authored material that still needs adoption. Implementation may not build it as canon.

**OPEN** — deliberately unresolved by the owner. No writer, artist or coding model may answer it, imply an answer, or plant evidence that only fits one answer.

When this document and an earlier package disagree about the opening or Act I, **this document governs** for the items it names in §2. Everything it does not name keeps its earlier status.

The owner adopted the narrative package **as direction**. Its scene treatment and dialogue are therefore the production draft. Timing and wording may be tuned in playtest. The meanings and boundaries in §3–§6 may not.

## 3. Locked creative rules from this pass

### 3.1 Rizo

- He is a small living flame: cute, funny, curious, vulnerable.
- **He is afraid of the dark.** He is also afraid of being left, being alone, the unknown, and moving forward when he cannot see what comes next. He loves home enough to keep going.
- He does not become brave. Over a long arc he becomes able to move while afraid. That arc is not resolved in Act I.
- His light is his face. Flame size, warmth and flicker carry emotion as much as his eyes do.
- **He understands tone, not words (N-14).** The player can understand the kidnappers. Rizo understands only that they are scared.

### 3.2 Rizo's words (N-08)

- At most **one** thought per act. Act I's is `home?`, at the awakening below.
- A thought is never speech and never explanation. It is a want or a fear.
- Presentation: no portrait, no name plate, lowercase, small, placed close to Rizo, fades on its own. It must not look like dialogue or narration.
- Later candidates (`stay.`, `warm.`, `light?`, `don't leave me.`) are PROPOSED only. None has a placement. Each may appear at most once in the campaign.

### 3.3 YOU

- A near-blank slate. No name, job, history or personality beyond warmth and ordinary competence.
- **YOU addresses the pet by its actual name (N-09).** The runtime already stores it as `campaign.petName`. A YOU line that names the pet uses that value. Where the code cannot supply it, the line falls back to "Rizo", never to a placeholder.
- **YOU leaves the light on (N-13).** YOU clicks the car's dome light on before leaving. This is a permanent relationship fact: YOU knows Rizo is afraid of the dark. Whether the final Den homecoming pays it off (for example with a night-light) is a separate, later OPEN decision.
- YOU never learns of the theft on screen in Act I. The last image of YOU is through glass, back turned, at the counter.

### 3.4 Tone

- The kidnappers are genuinely funny, and the situation is genuinely serious.
- **The turn rule.** Comedy may run until Rizo is hurt or alone. Then silence owns the next beat. Humor returns only through a new person or place.
- **Profanity ceiling (N-10):** RC2's existing "shit" is the maximum. A stronger word is cut off mid-word ("I said shut the f—").

### 3.5 The Boss (N-05, N-06)

Allowed in Act I:
- He is referred to only as "Boss," "him," or "he," and by the crew's fear.
- He calls rather than appears. A phone is his presence.
- Corporate texture through procedure: a job, a time limit, consequences for losing things, nobody saying no to him.
- **Accepted direction:** the crew misses a routine check-in by roughly a minute; they know that still matters. The Boss call follows soon after. This establishes active oversight without naming him, explaining his motive or linking him to BELOW.

Not allowed anywhere until the owner decides:
- A name, title beyond "Boss," company, organization, logo, building, uniform or face.
- Any reason for wanting Rizos.
- Any link between the Boss and BELOW. In the first campaign, nobody below knows him and nothing below explains him.

### 3.6 The caller symbol (N-07)

The phone's caller ID shows a single symbol and no text. It is never explained.

Constraints for whoever designs it:
- It is not a letter, word, name, initials, emoji face or company mark.
- It must not resemble Rizo's own "R" mark, any Rizo Apparel mark, or the blue of the Rizo Signal. Any of those would answer an OPEN question by accident.
- It must be reproducible exactly, because any later reappearance would become a deliberate plant.
- **Choosing the glyph is an owner/art decision.** Until then, scripts and tests refer to it as `CALLER_SYMBOL`.
- Reusing this symbol anywhere else in the game is forbidden until the owner decides what it means.

Small Hood's line "…It's him." is kept. He recognizes the symbol; the player does not learn why.

**Accepted opening continuity:** the phone Small Hood carries in the van is also the search flashlight on the roadside. During the crew's panicked retreat he drops that same phone. When `CALLER_SYMBOL` rings on it again after they leave, this is continuity of the existing Boss plant—not a new organization, motive, or BELOW connection.

## 4. The adopted opening (production draft)

Fourteen scenes, from the parked car to the first awakening below. Timings, triggers and control rules are fixed in the [beat sheet](Rizo-Dungeon-Opening-Beat-Sheet-v0.3.md). Targets: about **7 minutes** for a player who keeps moving, about **10** typical, about **15** for a player who lingers everywhere. Control is removed for only about 37 seconds in total.

| # | Scene | What must be true |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Parked | Ordinary warmth inside the car. Movement and interaction are learned by playing with YOU, not by instruction. |
| 2 | Be good | The accepted line, split so "Be good." lands alone. YOU turns the dome light on. THUNK. |
| 3 | Waiting | Nothing happens. YOU visible through the store window. The dome light times out. Curiosity lengthens normality and never shortens it. |
| 4 | Headlights | Rizo senses the van before the player sees it. A long silent hold before anything happens. |
| 5 | Taken | Flare makes them flinch and does not save him. His last sight is YOU, back turned, through glass. |
| 6 | The sack | Total dark except his own glow through fabric. |
| 7 | The van | Three movements: funny, uneasy, afraid of someone absent. The phone rings unanswered. |
| 8 | The gap | Rizo leans back from the dark; the player's held input carries him forward. He falls either way. |
| 9 | Taillights | The crew comes back. His light is now dangerous. He hides; they leave. Then total silence. |
| 10 | The ringing | Optional. A dropped phone glows in the grass, showing the caller symbol. |
| 11 | The walk | RC2's walk, nearly untouched. Light-pole pools; one passing car that is not YOU. |
| 12 | The drain | Shelter first. Then the drain goes back too far; warm air pulls him into the dark. |
| 13 | The fall | Slip, one recovery, slip again, falling in black, all sound cut, impact. The handheld locks **on impact**. |
| 14 | Awakening | A pinprick of flame regrowing. Rubble behind, grey far above. `home?` Then he finds the scratched HOME ↑ himself. |

- DRIVER: "I thought you said he didn't do that fire shit." · TALL HOOD: "I said probably." · DRIVER: "My bad. Pothole."
- Narration: "Lights go by. None of them are the store." · "The door isn't shut right. Rain comes through the gap." · "A bus stop. No bus." · "A bowl in the rain. Somebody's, once." / "Not his." · "A glove. Dry. Somebody waited here once." · "The way down filled in behind him." / "Up is the only way left." · "Someone scratched it with a key: HOME, and an arrow. Up."

### 4.2 Adopted dialogue draft

The full draft is section D of the narrative-direction package, with these owner adjustments:
- YOU's lines use the pet's actual name (N-09).
- Caller ID is `CALLER_SYMBOL`, not "him" (N-07). "…It's him." remains Small Hood's spoken line.
- "I said shut the f—" is cut off, as written (N-10).

The beat sheet reproduces every line in order with its timing, so implementers work from one source.

### 4.3 Meanings the dialogue must preserve

- Rizo was recognized and taken on purpose.
- The crew answers to someone they fear.
- The crew did not know about the fire.
- "One" in "the last dude who lost one" could mean anything.
- They brought a cooler "in case." What the case is stays OPEN.
- "Two minutes." times the grab to YOU's store trip. It must not settle whether they had watched YOU before.
- Nobody says why.

## 6. Choices and persistence

### 6.1 Rules (locked)

- Choices are actions with existing verbs. A menu appears only when the action is staying or going.
- No announcement, karma cue, meter, or "Rizo will remember that."
- Both sides cost something. A selfish choice may genuinely be easier.
- No choice may block the route home, the true homecoming, the pet's normal care, or a required outcome. A low-bond, untrained or quick player gets the same real ending.

### 6.2 Act I choices

**The ringing phone.** Touching it connects the call: whoever is on the line hears rain and a small breath, then hangs up. Leaving it lets it ring out. No narration either way.
- What a connected call means later is **OPEN**. It must be decided before any later chapter refers to the phone.
- The phone's comfort is the risk; enduring the dark is the safe choice. Nothing tells the player this.

**SIT / GO.** Unchanged in outcome. SIT gains a short quiet beat where nothing happens.

### 6.3 What would persist (not implemented now)

The Dungeon save already separates story facts, story choices, per-character state and committed beats. Its normalizer accepts only known facts and one choice (`hearth-seat`).

The only new persistent opening choice proposed is **`callerConnected`**: Rizo touched the phone and the call connected. It must persist because its consequence is deferred (OPEN). It is named here as a proposal for the save brief, not as schema.

Each new fact needs an allowlist entry, migration, and tests in a separately scheduled task. **No save code changes in this pass.**

**Owner rule: persistence must be earned by a payoff.** No opening interaction is reserved or proposed as a save fact unless an adopted, concrete later payoff requires it. The gap interaction (Scene 8) is preserved exactly as designed but is **not** persisted: whether Rizo went through under the player's push or was thrown by the jolt is presentation only. The opening's other new beats (car, sack, search, fall, awakening) are likewise presentation and resume points, not choices.

## 7. What remains OPEN

Preserved deliberately. Nothing in the opening may answer or narrow these.

- Why anyone wants Rizos.
- The Boss's identity, name, organization, motive and appearance.
- Whether Rizos are rare, valuable, or special in any way the player does not already know.
- What Rizos are, and any link to shipped hub lore (the Rizo Signal, the First Flame, the egg in the rain, the Deep Woods, the Void Den).
- Whether earlier Rizos were taken ("one").
- What the cooler was for.
- Whether the crew watched YOU before tonight.
- Whether the silent capped hood is closer to the Boss than the others, and whether he is the Driver (v0.2 already held this open).
- What the caller symbol means, and whether it ever appears again.
- What a connected call changes.
- Who scratched HOME ↑ (v0.1's Eda proposal remains optional).
- How present YOU's search is while Rizo is lost (v0.2 D-07).
- The Den payoff, if any, for "Light's on."
- All of v0.2 D-03 to D-10.

## 8. Production requirements derived from this addendum

These are constraints for later implementation briefs. They are not authorizations to implement.

1. **Opening resume points.** Players saved mid-opening in RC2 (beats `opening:taken`, `opening:fell`, `opening:below`) must resume sensibly when the car replaces the curb. Content revision `threshold-v1` will need a bump and a migration. A committed beat still never replays.
2. **Overheard talk uses barks, not blocking dialogue.** The van conversation and the roadside search are things Rizo overhears. They should play at their own pace in the existing non-blocking speech bubbles while Rizo keeps his limited control. This also stops the player tapping through them.
3. **The search needs a "seen by light" rule.** Being in a beam while not in shadow, or Flaring, draws attention. It never causes capture or failure.
4. **Thought-word presentation** is its own component, distinct from dialogue, narration and banners.
5. **No HOME ↑ banner on landing.** The sign is the existing inspectable in the Slip, found by Rizo's own light.
6. **The handheld lock moves to the moment of impact.** Its duration (560 ms) is unchanged.
7. **Reduced motion.** Every new camera hold, sweep and fall has a reduced-motion version, as RC2's existing scenes do.
8. **Sound-off legibility.** Every emotional beat in the beat sheet must read with sound muted. Silence is designed, not assumed.
